How these are computed — Portfolio beta = Σ(weight × ticker beta), from 3 yrs of weekly returns vs. SPY, computed in-repo. 30-day volatility = annualised stdev of daily returns. Weighted volatility is the naive Σ(wᵢ·σᵢ) — it deliberately ignores correlation, so it overstates true portfolio risk.

Sharpe = (portfolio return − risk-free) ÷ total volatility. Sortino = same excess return ÷ downside deviation only, so it doesn't penalise upside moves — generally the more useful of the two for a long-only book. Risk-free rate is the 3-month T-bill (DGS3MO) pulled from FRED, which is free and keyless.

⚠️ Both ratios inherit the correlation blind spot above. Because weighted vol overstates true portfolio risk, these Sharpe and Sortino figures are conservative — your real ratios are likely better. Computing the full covariance matrix fixes this and is free (we already pull 3 years of price history for beta); it's the top v1.1 item. Ratios also need ≥1 year of history, so the four recent listings are excluded from the calculation.

⚠️ What this tab is — and what it is not

This applies Buffett's publicly stated, written criteria — durable moat, high returns on capital, low leverage, consistent owner earnings, circle of competence, margin of safety — as a deterministic checklist against your holdings.

It is not a prediction of what Buffett would actually do, not affiliated with Berkshire, and not investment advice. It's a rules engine with his principles encoded — every verdict shows exactly which test drove it, so you can disagree with the rule rather than trust a black box.

No LLM is involved. That keeps it free, deterministic, reproducible, and auditable — and it never invents a rationale.
